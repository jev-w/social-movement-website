The Issue

What Is Youth Homelessness?

Youth homelessness in Ontario affects people aged 13 to 24. It's often invisible — which is exactly why it doesn't get enough attention.

Why It's Hard to See

Most people picture homelessness as someone sleeping outside, but a lot of homeless youth never end up on the street. They couch surf, sleep in cars, or stay in unsafe situations just to avoid shelters. Because it's hidden, people assume it's not a serious problem.

Who's Most Affected

  • Youth aging out of the foster care system
  • LGBTQ+ youth kicked out of home
  • Newcomers and refugees
  • Youth with mental health challenges
  • Low-income families in housing crisis

Why We Need to Talk About This

When a young person doesn't have stable housing, everything else falls apart too — school, jobs, mental health. The problem is also preventable. Ontario spends money responding to youth homelessness after the fact instead of stopping it from happening in the first place. More support for youth before they hit a crisis point would go a long way.

Julie's Story

Case Study — United Way Eastern Ontario

How the System Failed Julie

Julie grew up dealing with an unstable home life and was eventually placed in a group home through the foster care system. When she aged out of care, the support disappeared overnight. There was no plan, no transition help, just nothing.

She ended up couch surfing — constantly moving between friends and people she barely knew. Most of those situations were unsafe or emotionally abusive. Eventually she had no choice but to turn to shelters, all while dealing with anxiety, depression, and substance use on her own.

Julie's situation isn't rare. A lot of youth who grow up in the foster care system end up homeless not because of anything they did wrong, but because there's no real plan to support them when they turn 18. That's a gap our movement is focused on closing.

Source: United Way Eastern Ontario — "Help Youth Like Julie Get Off Ottawa's Streets"
